# European Spatiotemporal Geography

An interactive 3D **space–time cube** of Europe, North Africa and the Near East, from Iceland and Morocco to Persia and the Urals. A topographic relief map shows political boundaries at a chosen moment. It slides along a vertical time axis from 500 BCE to 2010, through the stacked boundary layers of 44 historical snapshots. 109 historic events are placed in the cube at their place and time, including day-dated campaigns of both world wars.

## Features

- **Space–time cube**: x/y is the map (equal-area projection centred on Europe) and z is time. Boundary layers for every snapshot are stacked up the time axis, faint when far from the current year.
- **3D topo slice**: the selected year is drawn on shaded relief terrain with adjustable vertical exaggeration.
- **Trace a polity**: click a country to highlight its footprint in every snapshot where it appears, as a column through time.
- **Historic events**: diamonds mark battles, treaties, religious and cultural turning points, and disasters at their place and year. The current period's events are labelled. Click one, in the cube or on the timeline, for a summary card that shows the borders in force at the time and links to Wikipedia. Arrows step through events in order.
- **Views**: Cube, Map (top-down) and Profile (side-on, time vertical). The time axis can be proportional to years or evenly spaced per snapshot.
- **Geographic names**: 88 seas, mountain ranges, deserts, rivers and cities, labelled in cartographic style in every language. Cities appear only once founded and carry the name of the era (Byzantium → Constantinople → Istanbul; Saint Petersburg → Petrograd → Leningrad). Minor names appear as you zoom in. Defined in `data/places.json`.
- **War focus (time lens)**: stretches 1914–1919 and/or 1938–1945 to a large share of the time axis. The war's day-dated events then spread out month by month above the borders of 1914 or 1938. A thread links them in date order, so you can follow the war across space and time. Translucent territory layers (6 phases for WWI, 8 for WWII) float at their dates inside the band. They show land occupied or annexed by the Central Powers/Axis, taken by the Entente/Allies, or annexed by the USSR in 1939–40. No openly licensed wartime border data exists, so `tools/build_war_layers.py` composes these from the 1914/1938 country outlines and simplified front lines. They are approximate. While a band is open, every phase and dated war event becomes a stop on the timeline. Dragging, the arrow keys and Play then step through the war, and the map slice shows that date's territory hatched over the pre-war borders.
- **12 languages**: English, 简体中文, 日本語, 한국어, Русский, Deutsch, Français, Español, Italiano, Português, Türkçe and العربية (right-to-left). Interface, periods and all event summaries are translated, and Wikipedia links open in the reader's language where an article exists. The language is picked from the browser, remembered, and can be shared with `#lang=de`. Polity names (535 historical states and peoples) are translated too.
- Timeline scrubbing, playback, keyboard control (← → step, space to play, Esc to clear), light and dark themes, and shareable links (`#year=1815`).

## Running locally

It's a static site, but it must be served over HTTP (not opened as a file):

```bash
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000>.

## Data

| Layer | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Historical boundaries | [aourednik/historical-basemaps](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps) | GPL-3.0 |
| Historic events | `data/events.json`, summaries written for this project, linking to Wikipedia | GPL-3.0 (this project) |
| Geographic names | `data/places.json`, compiled for this project | GPL-3.0 (this project) |
| Wartime territories | `data/war_layers.json`, derived by `tools/build_war_layers.py` (see [References](#references)) | GPL-3.0 (this project) |
| Elevation | [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) (Mapzen; SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1, EU-DEM and others) | see [attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md) |

`data/boundaries.json` and `data/terrain.png` are generated by `tools/build_data.py`. The script:

- clips each snapshot to Europe, North Africa and the Near East;
- reprojects to Lambert azimuthal equal-area (lon₀ 20°, lat₀ 48°);
- simplifies borders to about 2.5 km and drops fragments under 40 km²;
- gives each ruler (`SUBJECTO`) one colour across all eras, so neighbouring polities differ;
- resamples terrain to a 5 km grid, with a land mask taken from the 2010 coastline.

One source defect is repaired: Switzerland is a degenerate sliver in the 1994, 2000 and 2010 files, so its previous outline is reused.

Historical borders are approximate. Each snapshot is shown until the next one.

Translations live in `data/i18n/<lang>.json`, with `en.json` as the source. To add a language, copy `en.json`, translate the values (keep the keys and `{placeholders}`), and add the code to `LANGUAGES` in `js/app.js`. `tools/fetch_wiki_links.py` refreshes the localized Wikipedia titles stored in `data/events.json`.

To rebuild the data (needs `numpy`, `pillow`, `shapely`):

```bash
python tools/build_data.py --raw path/to/download-cache
```

## References

### Data sources

- **Historical boundaries.** Ourednik, A. *historical-basemaps: historical boundaries of polities as GeoJSON.* GitHub, https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps, licensed GPL-3.0, accessed October 2026. This project uses 44 snapshots (500 BCE – 2010). The upstream project describes itself as work in progress and asks users to verify its maps against other sources before academic use. It credits its own sources, among them the ThinkQuest Team C006628.
- **Elevation.** Mapzen / Tilezen Joerd, *Terrain Tiles* (Terrarium format), hosted by AWS Open Data: https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/. The tiles blend several public datasets; see the [attribution list](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md), which includes:
  - SRTM (NASA / USGS)
  - GMTED2010 (USGS / NGA)
  - ETOPO1 (NOAA)
  - EU-DEM. Produced using Copernicus data and information funded by the European Union – EU-DEM layers.
- **Wikipedia.** Each event links to its English Wikipedia article. Localized links come from the interlanguage links returned by the MediaWiki API (`tools/fetch_wiki_links.py`). No Wikipedia text is reproduced: the summaries are original. Dates follow the linked articles.

### Compiled for this project

These are original compilations, released under this repository's GPL-3.0 licence.

- **Historic events** (`data/events.json`): 109 events with place, date and a short summary.
- **Geographic names** (`data/places.json`): 88 seas, mountain ranges, deserts, rivers and cities, with approximate label positions, founding dates and historical city names (e.g. Byzantium → Constantinople → Istanbul).
- **Wartime territories** (`data/war_layers.json`, built by `tools/build_war_layers.py`): 6 WWI and 8 WWII phases. Each is composed from the 1914 and 1938 historical-basemaps outlines, cut along simplified front and demarcation lines. The lines were drawn for this project from general historical knowledge; they were not traced from a specific atlas. They are approximate at continental scale and are listed as editable coordinates in the script.
- **Translations** (`data/i18n/`): the 11 non-English languages were produced with AI assistance (Anthropic's Claude) and checked automatically for completeness. Native speakers have not reviewed them. Corrections are welcome.

### Methods

- **Space–time cube.** Hägerstrand, T. (1970). What about people in regional science? *Papers of the Regional Science Association* 24, 7–21. Kraak, M.-J. (2003). The space-time cube revisited from a geovisualization perspective. *Proceedings of the 21st International Cartographic Conference*, Durban, 1988–1996.
